# Design decisions

The look, the logo and the layout of the page, with what was tried and dropped on the way.
All of it can be revisited, but each point was settled with the reader, so ask before changing one.

- Fonts: GFS Didot (display, names, stories, Greek) + Manrope (interface).
- Look: a modern star chart after Hevelius (the default since the reader chose it; styles in `src/styles/stars.css`, over the tokens in `src/styles/tokens.css`). Clean ivory by day, graphite by night (a navy night sky with faint stars was tried; the reader did not like it, and chose graphite over a warm black). Follows the system setting until the reader picks a theme with the sun/moon button, which sits with the whole-tree button (in the top corner of the tree on desktop, in the line of pills on phones; the reader moved it there from the header); the pick is saved in localStorage (`ichor-theme`), and a pick that matches the system is not saved, so the page follows the system again.
  - Gold is ichor, the blood of the gods: transformations, the selected figure, Ariadne’s thread, focus (`--accent`). Red is for curses only.
  - Light: bg `#F5F2EB`, panel `#FCFAF6`, rule `#DDD5C6`, text `#18171B`, lines `#2E2D33`, muted `#5E574C`, curse `#AE2F1A`, gold `#8A5E00`.
  - Dark: bg `#161719`, panel `#1F2023`, rule `#35373C`, text `#ECEAE6`, lines `#B4B2AD`, muted `#A3A19C`, curse `#E8604A`, gold `#E8B54A`.
  - The earlier palette “pale clay and gold ichor” (light bg `#E8CFB2`, dark `#14100C`, medallion nodes) stays in `tokens.css` and shows with `?look=clay`; the antique atlas with `?look=atlas` (below).
- Logo (reworked with the star-chart look, after many rounds; the earlier iota wordmark, “ichor” with a gold breathing, was dropped: the reader wanted a fresh start tied to what ichor is): the mark is a four-rayed star, like the stars on the tree, whose south ray runs into a drop of gold: the light of the gods turns into their blood, and the stars recall the heroes they set in the sky. Its shape, settled step by step with the reader: side rays shorter than the upright ones (7 to 11), a small heart where the rays meet (fine cubic curves hugging the axes, not the fuller sparkle), a narrow drop whose fine, curved-in tip meets the south ray. Gold only, never red. The wordmark is the mark beside “Ichor” in GFS Didot, capital I (outlined from the font; a lower-case i was tried and the reader kept the capital), with the subtitle under it in the header (in the header itself the lockup differs, below). The mark is a little taller than the capitals (1.14 times) and aligned to their top: its north ray is level with the top of the I and the drop dips just below the line, as round letters do (chosen over the star centred on the letters, the whole mark centred, and a mark exactly as tall as the capitals). The favicon and app icons use the mark alone, drawn a little fuller (wider heart and drop) so it holds at 16px. The geometry is generated by a script (star and drop helpers, glyphs from GFS Didot via fontTools); in the header the lockup is “Ichor”, then the mark standing between the word and the subtitle, which runs beside it in two lines (“The family tree / of Greek myth”; the reader chose the mark over a hairline divider there, and the subtitle beside the word over under it); on phones the subtitle shows too (hidden below 360px wide), and the mark stands on the divider between the Family and Source cells below it (those two cells share the line evenly, so the divider does not move with the family’s name; the gap before the mark is computed in CSS from the screen width, so it is right from the first frame); the word and the mark are inline SVG in the header kicker (`.wordmark`, `.wm-sep`), coloured by `--text` and `--accent` (`.wm-ink`, `.wm-mark`); the files in `brand/` and `og.png` keep the mark before the word; sources in `brand/`, icons, manifest and the link card `og.png` in `public/`. Icons and the link card follow the star-chart palette (ivory `#F5F2EB`, ink `#18171B`, graphite `#161719` for the dark favicon). In running text the name stays “Ichor”.
- Small medallion nodes: gods have a double ring, figures from other cycles a dashed ring, a diamond badge marks a transformation, a small gold star marks a mortal made immortal (Semele, Cadmus, Helen, Menelaus, the Dioscuri, Iphigenia, Heracles, Ino, Ganymede, Tithonus, Ariadne, Amphiaraus, Pegasus, Io, Memnon; it hides when the chosen source doesn’t tell it), a small peak marks one of the Twelve Olympians (hollow for Hestia and Dionysus); a group (the Muses, the Fates…) is a ringed medallion holding three small dots.
- Row names sit in a column pinned to the left edge of the screen on desktop (small caps, interface font, fixed size at any zoom); the tree slides beneath it and the names follow their rows up and down. The “Heroes and mortals” divider is named there too. On phones there is no column (it took too much width) and no framed tags: each name is written on its row’s top rule, in small caps, at the start of the line, and moves with the tree, so it shows only when the left end of the tree is in view (the reader chose this over names that wait at the screen edge). The divider’s name sits just above its line. When the tree is zoomed far out (the whole tree) the rows are too close for names, so they hide.
- Star-atlas look (a mockup, only with `?look=atlas`, styles in `src/styles/atlas.css`; `&frame=none` hides the border): after Hevelius’ Uranographia (1690). Light = old paper (grain, foxing, a darker edge; laid and chain lines were tried and dropped, the reader did not like the stripes), dark = the night sky. Figures are stars as Hevelius drew them: a bright one (gods, and figures with many links) a dark seal with a jagged rim and a pale star inside, a faint one a small six-rayed star; a group three small stars. Names in IM Fell English (17th-century type): mortals in italic, gods and groups in small capitals. The border is Hevelius’ chequered band between two rules. The logo stays as it is. Under discussion with the reader: she was unsure of the border and asked for other stars and more paper (second round). The modern variant, now the site’s default look, keeps Hevelius’ idea without the antique dress (`src/styles/stars.css`): clean ivory by day, graphite by night, no frame and no paper; a bright figure is a four-pointed sparkle in a fine ring, a faint one the sparkle alone; mortals in GFS Didot, gods, groups and section heads in spaced Manrope capitals; hairline links (a marriage is two hairlines, a wider line with a band of the background down its middle: a single hairline for every link had hidden marriages behind that band; the links of a selected figure, a story step, the thread or a Guide example come forward slightly thicker). The rows stay (the reader asked whether a star chart needs them; they carry “lower means descended” and fixed places across sources), drawn as a chart’s graticule: dotted parallels with graduations, faint dotted meridians every 240px, the gods–mortals divider ruled and graduated like the ecliptic, and row numerals on desktop with a short tick like a margin scale. Buttons are square, like the cells of a table (the reader asked: rounded pills did not fit the star chart); buttons that belong together share their rules: the source pills, the theme switch and the whole-tree button, the card navigator, the phone tools bar and the phone line of pills. On phones those two lines are rows of a table rather than boxes: the line of pills is ruled above and below, with hairline dividers between its cells running the full height of the row, and the tools are a strip along the bottom edge, ruled on top, with dividers (the reader suggested dividers inside the line instead of boxes inside a box). The Guide button is a cell of the header too, as wide as the theme and whole-tree cells below it, its divider running into theirs (the reader asked). The header icons are hairlines in the same spirit: the moon a sparkle beside it, the sun is the astronomer’s ☉ (a ring with a dot) with eight short rays, and the whole-tree button is four corners round a sparkle. The engraving in In the sky is shown as a line drawing (ink multiplied into the ivory, or inverted to light lines on the graphite); a tap shows the plate as printed.
- Edge styles: descent = thin solid; marriage = double line; affair = dashed; feud = dash-dot; transformation = dotted gold with arrow; curse = red with arrow.
- Header (reworked in task 4; the meander band was dropped): the wordmark with the subtitle “The family tree of Greek myth” under it (display face, muted; it sat beside the wordmark in small caps before), then the family pill. The family’s title ("Gods and heroes" for the whole tree) stays as the page’s h1 for screen readers and search but is no longer shown, since the Family pill already names it (the reader asked to drop the line under the logo). The title is only a title: the family switcher is a pill on its own line, “Family [Whole tree ▾]”, just above the Source pills and labelled the same way, so both choices read as settings (chosen over a “Change family” button beside the title and over a framed title-button; the old “Now exploring” line and the chevron on the title were not obvious). On phones the Family and Source labels stay visible and the list opens full width; there the sources are a pill too, “Source [All versions ▾]”, opening a list with each group’s authors, instead of a row of pills that had to be scrolled. The families: Whole tree, The gods, The Titans, Zeus and his loves, Thebes, The House of Atreus, Perseus and Heracles, The Argonauts, Troy, The children of Heracles, Theseus and Athens, The Odyssey, The Seven against Thebes, Bellerophon and Corinth, The house of Minos, Io and the Danaids, Monsters and their killers, Orpheus and the underworld, The Trojan War (`families` in `data/branches.json`; the choice lives in the URL, `?family=thebes`). Everyone goes into the one pool of data; families keep the tree readable as it grows. Collectives are one medallion each (`kind: group`): the Muses, Moirai, Horae, Charites, Oceanids, Pleiades, Erinyes, the sons of Megara, the suitors of Penelope, the companions of Odysseus, the Danaids and the sons of Aegyptus, the Graeae, the Gorgons, the Nereids, the Maenads, the bandits of the Isthmus road, the Harpies, the Sirens, the Cyclopes (Hesiod’s three smiths, not the Cyclopes of the Odyssey) and the Hundred-Handers; the card lists the sisters. Sources are pills, the chosen one filled; on desktop their note sits on the same line.
- Card bar: the card has a bar on top that stays in view as it scrolls, with a close button (×, replacing the “Close” text that scrolled away) and the figure’s or panel’s name, which appears once the big name scrolls off. On phones the bar is also the grip: pull up for full height, down to shrink or close; the sheet opens at about 62% of the screen. A card with three sections or more has a navigator under the bar: a scrolling row of short section names (About, Family, Stories, Parents, Married, Affairs, Feuds, Children, Versions, Not ancient, Art…); tapping one scrolls there, and the section being read is marked. The header also folds once the reader moves the tree (pan or zoom, not a tap), and comes back with “Whole tree” or a new family. On phones the header is one line: the wordmark and a Guide button (a word; it was an “i” icon, then “About”; the reader chose “Guide”, since the panel is mostly how to read the tree); the hint (“Lower means descended, not later”), the line styles and the source groups live in the Guide panel (“Guide to Ichor”) it opens in the card sheet. Below it one line holds the Family and Source pills (no labels; long names are cut with an ellipsis), then the theme switch and the whole-tree button as an icon (four corners), so it does not repeat the “Whole tree” family name. The source note shows for a few seconds under that line after a source is picked. While a card is open on a phone, the one-line header folds away and only the line of pills stays, led by the logo’s mark (the star and its drop, in gold; the reader asked to keep it in view), so the tree gets the room above the card; when the card closes, the header comes back and the view stays centred on what was shown (the reader found the tree cramped and closing a long card meant scrolling back up); only what the camera framed above the card glides to the centre (a figure, a story step, the thread, a Guide example or a tour step): a plain panel (Layers, Find, the list of Stories, an empty thread) leaves the tree where it is on screen, since it never moved it (each opening and closing had pushed the tree 200px further down); only when the sheet would leave no figure in the band above it (figures at its very edge don’t count) do the nearest figures glide up into the band (`keepInView`), so the reader never sees an empty tree above a panel. The fold is animated on phones (`foldMotion` in `sheet.js`; the reader found the jump too abrupt): the header fades and lifts away (a copy of it, while the real one is already gone), the line of pills slides up into its place, and the gold mark flies from its place in the header to the start of the line, the family name moving aside to make room; coming back plays it in reverse, the words of the header fading in. Only transform and opacity move, so the motion holds while the tree is busy; it starts on the next free frame. The tools strip sits outside `.controls` in the HTML, so moving the line never drags the strip along. With reduced motion, and on desktop, the header simply switches. Desktop has the same Guide button in the top right corner of the header, opening the Guide in the card. The Guide is a live legend (the reader asked for it to be interactive): each line style can be tapped to show one example on the tree (Atreus and Agamemnon, Zeus and Hera, Zeus and Leda, Atreus and Thyestes, Zeus and Io, Myrtilus and Pelops, or the best-attested link of that kind in the family shown), with its sentence and sources; “Lower means descended, not later” shows Agamemnon and Diomedes, three rows apart though they fought side by side (when both don’t fit at a readable size, the camera shows Atreus and Agamemnon, then glides to Tydeus and Diomedes); each source can be tapped to switch the tree to it, with its note under it, and tapped again for all versions. An example a family does not hold says so. “Take the tour →” at the top of the Guide (the reader asked for it) opens “A minute with Ichor”, five steps on the whole tree (it switches there from a family), each lit up and framed like a step of a story, with Back, dots and Next: the stars (Zeus and Hera); the lines (the house of Atreus: Pelops, Aerope, Thyestes, Agamemnon, with the curse); lower means descended (Agamemnon and Diomedes, with the glide); the sources (Agamemnon’s fathers, with All versions / Homer & epic / Hesiod to switch); Stories and the thread (Oedipus to Orestes through Pelops’ curse on Laius), ending with Open Stories, Try the thread and Done. The theme switch stays left of the whole-tree button, which keeps the corner as the more used of the two. The rows were renamed from “Titans” and “Children of the Titans” to “Titans and their kin” and “Zeus and his kin”, since those rows also hold the Cyclopes, the Hundred-Handers, the Erinyes, Pontus, Hypnos, Thanatos and Eris (Hesiod’s Titans are only the twelve, Theogony 133–138), and Nemesis, Typhon and Nereus, who are not children of Titans.
