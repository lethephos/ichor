# Design

The look, the logo and the layout of the page as they are now. Each point was settled with the reader, so ask before changing one.

## Look

- A modern star chart after Hevelius’ Uranographia (1690), without antique dress: clean ivory by day, graphite by night, no frame and no paper. Colours are tokens in `src/styles/tokens.css`; the rest of the look is in `src/styles/stars.css`.
- Fonts: GFS Didot (display, names, stories, Greek) and Manrope (interface). Mortals are set in GFS Didot; gods, groups and section heads in spaced Manrope capitals.
- Gold is ichor, the blood of the gods: transformations, the selected figure, Ariadne’s thread, focus (`--accent`). Red is for curses only.
  - Light: bg `#F5F2EB`, panel `#FCFAF6`, rule `#DDD5C6`, text `#18171B`, lines `#2E2D33`, muted `#5E574C`, curse `#AE2F1A`, gold `#8A5E00`.
  - Dark: bg `#161719`, panel `#1F2023`, rule `#35373C`, text `#ECEAE6`, lines `#B4B2AD`, muted `#A3A19C`, curse `#E8604A`, gold `#E8B54A`.
- The theme follows the system setting until the reader picks one with the sun/moon button. The pick is saved in localStorage (`ichor-theme`); a pick that matches the system is not saved, so the page follows the system again.

## Figures and links

- A bright figure (a god, or a figure with many links) is a four-pointed sparkle in a fine ring; a faint one is the sparkle alone; a group (the Muses, the Fates…) is three small stars.
- Marks beside a figure: a diamond badge for a transformation; a small gold star for a mortal made immortal (Semele, Cadmus, Helen, Menelaus, the Dioscuri, Iphigenia, Heracles, Ino, Ganymede, Tithonus, Ariadne, Amphiaraus, Pegasus, Io, Memnon), which hides when the chosen source doesn’t tell it; a small peak for one of the Twelve Olympians, hollow for Hestia and Dionysus.
- Links are hairlines. Descent = thin solid; marriage = double line (two hairlines: a wider line with a band of the background down its middle); affair = dashed; feud = dash-dot; transformation = dotted gold with arrow; curse = red with arrow. The links of a selected figure, a story step, the thread or a Guide example come forward slightly thicker.
- Collectives are one figure each (`kind: group`): the Muses, Moirai, Horae, Charites, Oceanids, Pleiades, Erinyes, the sons of Megara, the suitors of Penelope, the companions of Odysseus, the Danaids and the sons of Aegyptus, the Graeae, the Gorgons, the Nereids, the Maenads, the bandits of the Isthmus road, the Harpies, the Sirens, the Cyclopes (Hesiod’s three smiths, not the Cyclopes of the Odyssey) and the Hundred-Handers. The card lists the members.

## Rows

- The rows carry “lower means descended” and keep every figure in a fixed place across sources. They are drawn as a chart’s graticule: dotted parallels with graduations, faint dotted meridians every 240px, the gods–mortals divider ruled and graduated like the ecliptic.
- On desktop the row names sit in a column pinned to the left edge of the screen (small caps, interface font, fixed size at any zoom), with a short tick like a margin scale; the tree slides beneath and the names follow their rows up and down. The “Heroes and mortals” divider is named there too.
- On phones there is no column: each name is written on its row’s top rule, in small caps, at the start of the line, and moves with the tree, so it shows only when the left end of the tree is in view. The divider’s name sits just above its line. When the tree is zoomed far out the rows are too close for names, so they hide.
- The rows of the gods are named “The first gods”, “Titans and their kin”, “Zeus and his kin” and “The younger gods”: “and their kin” because those rows also hold the Cyclopes, the Hundred-Handers, the Erinyes, Pontus, Hypnos, Thanatos and Eris (Hesiod’s Titans are only the twelve, Theogony 133–138), and Nemesis, Typhon and Nereus, who are not children of Titans.

## Logo

- The mark is a four-rayed star, like the stars on the tree, whose south ray runs into a drop of gold: the light of the gods turns into their blood, and the stars recall the heroes they set in the sky. Gold only, never red.
- Its shape: side rays shorter than the upright ones (7 to 11), a small heart where the rays meet (fine cubic curves hugging the axes), a narrow drop whose fine, curved-in tip meets the south ray.
- The wordmark is the mark beside “Ichor” in GFS Didot with a capital I, outlined from the font. The mark is a little taller than the capitals (1.14 times) and aligned to their top: its north ray is level with the top of the I and the drop dips just below the line, as round letters do.
- The favicon and app icons use the mark alone, drawn a little fuller (wider heart and drop) so it holds at 16px. Icons and the link card follow the palette (ivory `#F5F2EB`, ink `#18171B`, graphite `#161719` for the dark favicon).
- The geometry is generated by a script (star and drop helpers, glyphs from GFS Didot via fontTools). Sources are in `brand/`; icons, manifest and the link card `og.png` in `public/`. The files in `brand/` and `og.png` put the mark before the word.
- In running text the name is “Ichor”.

## Header

- The lockup in the header is “Ichor”, then the mark standing between the word and the subtitle, which runs beside it in two lines (“The family tree / of Greek myth”). The word and the mark are inline SVG in the header kicker (`.wordmark`, `.wm-sep`), coloured by `--text` and `--accent` (`.wm-ink`, `.wm-mark`).
- The family’s title (“Gods and heroes” for the whole tree) is the page’s h1 for screen readers and search, and is not shown: the Family pill names it.
- The family switcher is a pill on its own line, “Family [Whole tree ▾]”, just above the Source pills and labelled the same way, so both choices read as settings. The choice lives in the address (`?family=thebes`).
- Sources are pills, the chosen one filled; on desktop their note sits on the same line.
- The Guide button is a cell of the header, in its top right corner, as wide as the theme and whole-tree cells below it, its divider running into theirs.
- “Whole tree” sits in the top corner of the tree with the theme switch to its left; while a card is open they move to the left of the card.
- Buttons are square, like the cells of a table. Buttons that belong together share their rules: the source pills, the theme switch and the whole-tree button, the card navigator, the phone tools strip and the phone line of pills.
- The header icons are hairlines: the moon with a sparkle beside it, the sun as the astronomer’s ☉ (a ring with a dot) with eight short rays, the whole-tree button as four corners round a sparkle.

## The header on phones

- The header is one line: the wordmark, the subtitle (hidden below 360px wide) and the Guide button.
- Below it one line holds the Family and Source pills (no labels; long names are cut with an ellipsis; each opens a full-width list, the sources with each group’s authors), then the theme switch and the whole-tree button as an icon. The mark stands on the divider between the Family and Source cells; those two cells share the line evenly, so the divider does not move with the family’s name, and the gap before the mark is computed in CSS from the screen width.
- That line and the tools are rows of a table rather than boxes: the line of pills is ruled above and below, with hairline dividers between its cells running the full height of the row; the tools (Layers, Stories, Thread, Find) are a strip along the bottom edge, ruled on top, with dividers. The tools strip sits outside `.controls` in the HTML, so moving the line never drags the strip along.
- The source note shows for a few seconds under the line after a source is picked.
- The hint (“Lower means descended, not later”), the line styles and the source groups live in the Guide.
- The header folds away once the reader moves the tree (pan or zoom, not a tap) and while a card is open; only the line of pills stays, led by the mark in gold, so the tree gets the room above the card. It comes back with “Whole tree”, a new family, or when the card closes.
- The fold is animated (`foldMotion` in `sheet.js`): the header fades and lifts away (a copy of it, while the real one is already gone), the line of pills slides up into its place, and the gold mark flies from its place in the header to the start of the line, the family name moving aside to make room; coming back plays it in reverse. Only transform and opacity move, and the motion starts on the next free frame. With reduced motion, and on desktop, the header simply switches.

## The card

- A bar on top stays in view as the card scrolls, with a close button (×) and the figure’s or panel’s name, which appears once the big name scrolls off.
- On phones the bar is also the grip: pull up for full height, down to shrink or close. The sheet opens at about 62% of the screen.
- A card with three sections or more has a navigator under the bar: a scrolling row of short section names (About, Family, Feuds, Versions, Stories, Not ancient, Sky, Art…); tapping one scrolls there, and the section being read is marked.
- When a card closes on a phone, the view stays centred on what was shown. Only what the camera framed above the card glides to the centre (a figure, a story step, the thread, a Guide example or a tour step); a plain panel (Layers, Find, the list of Stories, an empty thread) leaves the tree where it is on screen. Only when a panel would leave no figure in the band above it (figures at its very edge don’t count) do the nearest figures glide up into the band (`keepInView`).
- The engraving in “In the sky” is shown as a line drawing (ink multiplied into the ivory, or inverted to light lines on the graphite); a tap shows the plate as printed.

## The Guide

- The Guide (“Guide to Ichor”) opens in the card. It is a live legend: each line style can be tapped to show one example on the tree (Atreus and Agamemnon, Zeus and Hera, Zeus and Leda, Atreus and Thyestes, Zeus and Io, Myrtilus and Pelops, or the best-attested link of that kind in the family shown), with its sentence and sources. An example a family does not hold says so.
- “Lower means descended, not later” shows Agamemnon and Diomedes, three rows apart though they fought side by side; when both don’t fit at a readable size, the camera shows Atreus and Agamemnon, then glides to Tydeus and Diomedes.
- Each source can be tapped to switch the tree to it, with its note under it, and tapped again for all versions.
- “Take the tour →” at the top opens “A minute with Ichor”, five steps on the whole tree (it switches there from a family), each lit up and framed like a step of a story, with Back, dots and Next: the stars (Zeus and Hera); the lines (the house of Atreus: Pelops, Aerope, Thyestes, Agamemnon, with the curse); lower means descended (Agamemnon and Diomedes, with the glide); the sources (Agamemnon’s fathers, with All versions / Homer & epic / Hesiod to switch); Stories and the thread (Oedipus to Orestes through Pelops’ curse on Laius), ending with Open Stories, Try the thread and Done.

## Tools

- On desktop the layer toggles, the Stories list, Ariadne’s thread and the search box sit in a dock at the bottom of the tree.
- On phones the four tools open panels in the card sheet. During a story the tools strip steps aside, so Back and Next are never under it.
