# The tree: rows, houses and families

Who sits where, and why. This is the record of what the data holds; `CLAUDE.md` has the rules.

The first branch is **"Two cursed houses"**: the House of Atreus and the House of Labdacus (Thebes), linked through Pelops and Chrysippus. Above them sit the gods (checked against Hesiod, Homer, the Homeric Hymns, Pindar, Euripides, Apollodorus, Pausanias, Hyginus and Ovid): so the tree runs from Gaia and Uranus down to Orestes.

Rows: The first gods, Titans and their kin (all twelve of Hesiod, with the Erinyes, and their brothers the Cyclopes and the Hundred-Handers, two groups of three), Zeus and his kin (Zeus and his siblings, the sons of Iapetus, the children of Hyperion, Crius and Coeus, and the goddesses Zeus loved; Nemesis, a daughter of Night, sits there as his lover; Atlas sits there too, with his daughters the Pleiades on a line just below him), The younger gods (the children of Zeus, with Hecate, granddaughter of Coeus; not “Children of Zeus”, since Hecate is not his, and in Hesiod neither are Aphrodite and Hephaestus, and the Moirai are also daughters of Night); then a “Heroes and mortals” divider (not “Mortals”: Harmonia is a goddess, and Semele, Polydeuces, Helen and Iphigenia become immortal in some sources) and rows I–VII. These rows count descent within each house, not time, so they are labelled with bare numerals, not “Generation”: the houses don’t line up (Cadmus to the Epigoni is six steps, his sister Europa to Agamemnon four), so Diomedes sits three rows below Agamemnon, whom he fought beside. The rows are not aligned by time: that would break either “siblings share a row” or “lower means descended”. The hint says so: “Lower means descended, not later.”

Thebes runs Cadmus and Harmonia (I), Polydorus, Agave and Semele (II), Labdacus and Pentheus (III), down to Eteocles and his sisters (VI); Semele’s son Dionysus stays with the gods, so his bloodline is the one drawn upwards.

The house of Perseus runs Acrisius and Danaë (I), Perseus, Andromeda and Medusa, with Polydectes of Seriphos and the sea monster (II), the sons of Perseus with Nicippe, a daughter of Pelops, and Amphitryon and Alcmene on a line below (III), down to Heracles, Eurystheus and Iphicles (IV), the row of Agamemnon, with those Heracles fought on his labours: Augeas, Diomedes of Thrace and the centaur Nessus beside him, Hippolyta, queen of the Amazons, on III: Eurystheus is a grandson of Pelops, born on the same day as Heracles. Creon, Jocasta’s brother, links it to Thebes through his daughter Megara.

The Argonauts run from Aeolus (I) through Salmoneus, Cretheus, Athamas, Ino and Tyro (II), Phrixus and Helle, Pelias, Aeson and Aeetes (III), to Jason, Medea, Absyrtus, Glauce, Hypsipyle and Orpheus (IV), with Hylas, Heracles’ beloved, and the serpent that guards the fleece; the blind seer Phineus (son of Agenor or of Poseidon, not the brother of Cepheus) sits on Generation I below Agenor, plagued by the Harpies (a group, daughters of the Oceanid Electra, younger gods); Aeetes and Circe are children of Helios, Ino is Cadmus’ daughter, and Heracles, the Dioscuri and Orpheus sail on the Argo.

Troy, the house of Dardanus, runs Dardanus, son of Zeus and the Pleiad Electra, with his son Erichthonius on a line below (I), Tros with Ilus, Assaracus and Ganymede below (II), Laomedon and Capys with Priam, Hecuba, Tithonus, Hesione and Anchises below (III), to Hector, Paris, Cassandra, Aeneas and their kin (IV), the row of Agamemnon, and Astyanax (V). Aeacus, Peleus and Thetis lead to Achilles, and his son Neoptolemus marries Hermione, so Troy meets the house of Atreus through Helen, Cassandra and Hermione.

The children of Heracles sit on Generation V: Hyllus and Macaria (by Deianira), the sons of Megara (a group: three in Apollodorus and Euripides, eight in Pindar), Telephus, Tlepolemus, and Iolaus, son of Iphicles; Iole and Omphale join Heracles’ row. Hyllus, Iolaus and Alcmene take their revenge on Eurystheus, and Achilles wounds and heals Telephus.

Athens runs the earth-born Erichthonius (child of Hephaestus and Gaia, raised by Athena), with his son Pandion below him, and Europa (I); Erechtheus, Procne, Philomela and Tereus, the later Cecrops and Pandion, Minos and Pasiphae (II); Aegeus, Aethra, Itys, Androgeus, Ariadne, Phaedra, the Minotaur, Antiope and Pirithous, and the bandits of the Isthmus road (a group), with Theseus on a line below (III); Hippolytus and Demophon (IV). Theseus links to the Argonauts through Medea, Aegeus’ wife, and to Atreus through Helen, whom he carried off.

The Odyssey runs Sisyphus (son of Aeolus), Autolycus (son of Hermes), Arcesius and Nausithous (II); Laertes and Anticleia, Icarius, Alcinous and Arete, and Telamon, son of Aeacus, with Hesione (III); Odysseus, Penelope, Nausicaa, Palamedes, Ajax, Polyphemus (son of Poseidon) and the companions of Odysseus (a group) (IV); Telemachus, Telegonus (his son by Circe) and the suitors of Penelope (a group) (V). Calypso, daughter of Atlas, sits on a line below him. The Sirens (a group, daughters of a Muse) and Scylla (daughter of Phorcys, or of Typhon and Echidna; a nymph Circe turned into a monster in Ovid) sit with the younger gods. Sisyphus is drawn as a second father of Odysseus, as Euripides, Sophocles and Hyginus tell it. A group’s card heads its member list with `count` and `members_label` (“A hundred and eight suitors”; “sisters” by default).

The Seven against Thebes adds Calydon, Oeneus and Althaea (III) with Meleager, Deianira’s brother, and Atalanta (IV); Adrastus, Amphiaraus and Eriphyle, Tiresias and Haemon, son of Creon (V); the Seven with Polynices on Generation VI (Tydeus, Capaneus, Parthenopaeus) with Argia, Deipyle and Evadne; and a new row, Generation VII, for the Epigoni: Thersander, Diomedes (who wounds Aphrodite and Ares at Troy) and Alcmaeon.

Bellerophon runs from Sisyphus through Glaucus of Corinth (on a line below him) and Iobates of Lycia (II), with Pegasus on a line below Medusa; Bellerophon, Philonoe, Proetus, Stheneboea and the Chimera (III); Hippolochus and Laodamia (IV); Glaucus of Lycia and Sarpedon, son of Zeus (V), who fight for Troy. Patroclus, Achilles’ friend, joins the row of Hector and kills Sarpedon.

The house of Minos adds Asterion, Europa’s husband (I); Rhadamanthys, who marries Alcmene, Daedalus and Cocalus (II), with Catreus and Deucalion on a line below Minos; Glaucus and the seer Polyidus, Icarus and Perdix, Talos, and Catreus’ children Clymene and Althaemenes, with Nauplius (III); Idomeneus (IV). Aerope is Catreus’ daughter, so Crete reaches the house of Atreus; Clymene and Nauplius are the parents of Palamedes.

Io and the Danaids runs from the river Inachus, son of Ocean (Zeus and his kin), down a chain of lines inside Generation I: Io and Argus; Epaphus; Libya; Belus and Agenor (sons of Poseidon); Danaus, Aegyptus, Cepheus, Phineus, with Cadmus and Europa, Agenor’s children; the Danaids and the sons of Aegyptus (two groups of fifty), Hypermnestra, Lynceus and Amymone; Abas; Acrisius; Danaë. Amymone is the mother of Nauplius, Cepheus and Cassiopeia the parents of Andromeda; Proetus, Acrisius’ twin, stays on the row of Bellerophon. Generation I is tall in the whole tree because of this chain.

The monsters adds Tartarus (first gods) and Pontus (Titans and their kin); Nereus, Phorcys, Ceto and Typhon (Zeus and his kin; Typhon drops a line below Hera, who bears him alone in the Homeric Hymn to Apollo); Echidna, the Graeae, the Gorgons, the Nereids (groups) and Ladon (younger gods); Chrysaor below Medusa (II); Orthus and Geryon beside the Chimera (III); the Nemean Lion, the Hydra and Cerberus on the row of Heracles (IV); the Sphinx on the row of Oedipus (V). Parentage of the monsters is left as the sources give it, with Hesiod’s unclear ‘she’ noted on the link.

Orpheus and the underworld adds Erebus, Night’s husband (first gods), her sons Hypnos and Thanatos (Titans and their kin), Tityos, son of Gaia (younger gods); Ixion, father of Pirithous (II); Oeagrus, Orpheus’ father, Pheres, son of Cretheus, and the Maenads (a group: Agave, Ino, Autonoe) (III); Eurydice, Admetus and Alcestis, daughter of Pelias (IV). The punishments in Hades (Tantalus, Sisyphus, Ixion, Tityos) are feuds with the god who imposed them.

The Trojan War adds Eris, daughter of Night (Titans and their kin row), whose apple brings Hera and Athena’s hatred of Paris; Neleus, Pelias’ twin, Poeas, Oileus, Chryses and Eetion (III); Nestor with Antilochus below him, Memnon, son of Eos and Tithonus, Penthesilea, daughter of Ares, Philoctetes, Ajax the Lesser, Chryseis, Briseis, Helenus and Laocoon (IV), with the killings of Memnon, Penthesilea, Paris, Astyanax and the two Ajaxes’ fates.

Prometheus and the fire adds Pandora, Epimetheus’ wife (Zeus and his kin), and Deucalion of the flood (not the Cretan Deucalion), his wife Pyrrha and their son Hellen, father of Aeolus (with the younger gods): so the Titans reach the house of the Argonauts.

Rows show a figure’s place in the story rather than a strict generation (Aphrodite, born of Uranus in Hesiod, sits with the younger gods).

The Olympians are not a row: they span two, and are marked instead by a small peak beside the medallion (`data/olympians.json`). No poet in our source groups lists all twelve, so the list follows the Parthenon east frieze (Dionysus) and Ennius (Vesta = Hestia), with Plato’s Phaedrus for Hestia; the twelfth seat is hollow. The idea that Hestia gave her seat to Dionysus is modern (Robert Graves, The Greek Myths, 1955, 27.k); it appears only as a “Not in the ancient sources” note on both cards, never as a link or a version.
