# Ichor — project brief

## What this is

**Ichor** is an interactive family tree of Greek mythology for an English-speaking audience.
Subtitle: *The family tree of Greek myth* (gods and mortals alike, so not “of the Greek gods”).

It shows not only who descends from whom, but marriages, affairs, feuds and killings, transformations and curses. Its main difference from existing family trees is the **versions layer**: every link records which ancient sources tell it, and the user can switch source (Homer, Hesiod, the tragedians, Apollodorus, later writers) and watch the family rearrange itself.

The first branch is **"Two cursed houses"**: the House of Atreus and the House of Labdacus (Thebes), linked through Pelops and Chrysippus. Above them sit the gods (checked against Hesiod, Homer, the Homeric Hymns, Pindar, Euripides, Apollodorus, Pausanias, Hyginus and Ovid): so the tree runs from Gaia and Uranus down to Orestes. Rows: The first gods, Titans (all twelve of Hesiod, with the Erinyes, and their brothers the Cyclopes and the Hundred-Handers, two groups of three), Children of the Titans (Zeus and his siblings, the sons of Iapetus, the children of Hyperion, Crius and Coeus, and the goddesses Zeus loved; Nemesis, a daughter of Night, sits there as his lover; Atlas sits there too, with his daughters the Pleiades on a line just below him), The younger gods (the children of Zeus, with Hecate, granddaughter of Coeus; not “Children of Zeus”, since Hecate is not his, and in Hesiod neither are Aphrodite and Hephaestus, and the Moirai are also daughters of Night); then a “Heroes and mortals” divider (not “Mortals”: Harmonia is a goddess, and Semele, Polydeuces, Helen and Iphigenia become immortal in some sources) and rows I–VII. These rows count descent within each house, not time, so they are labelled with bare numerals, not “Generation”: the houses don’t line up (Cadmus to the Epigoni is six steps, his sister Europa to Agamemnon four), so Diomedes sits three rows below Agamemnon, whom he fought beside. A time-aligned layout was considered and rejected: it would have to break either “siblings share a row” or “lower means descended”. The hint says so: “Lower means descended, not later.” Thebes runs Cadmus and Harmonia (I), Polydorus, Agave and Semele (II), Labdacus and Pentheus (III), down to Eteocles and his sisters (VI); Semele’s son Dionysus stays with the gods, so his bloodline is the one drawn upwards. The house of Perseus runs Acrisius and Danaë (I), Perseus, Andromeda and Medusa, with Polydectes of Seriphos and the sea monster (II), the sons of Perseus with Nicippe, a daughter of Pelops, and Amphitryon and Alcmene on a line below (III), down to Heracles, Eurystheus and Iphicles (IV), the row of Agamemnon, with those Heracles fought on his labours: Augeas, Diomedes of Thrace and the centaur Nessus beside him, Hippolyta, queen of the Amazons, on III: Eurystheus is a grandson of Pelops, born on the same day as Heracles. Creon, Jocasta’s brother, links it to Thebes through his daughter Megara. The Argonauts run from Aeolus (I) through Salmoneus, Cretheus, Athamas, Ino and Tyro (II), Phrixus and Helle, Pelias, Aeson and Aeetes (III), to Jason, Medea, Absyrtus, Glauce, Hypsipyle and Orpheus (IV), with Hylas, Heracles’ beloved, and the serpent that guards the fleece; the blind seer Phineus (son of Agenor or of Poseidon, not the brother of Cepheus) sits on Generation I below Agenor, plagued by the Harpies (a group, daughters of the Oceanid Electra, younger gods); Aeetes and Circe are children of Helios, Ino is Cadmus’ daughter, and Heracles, the Dioscuri and Orpheus sail on the Argo. Troy, the house of Dardanus, runs Dardanus, son of Zeus and the Pleiad Electra, with his son Erichthonius on a line below (I), Tros with Ilus, Assaracus and Ganymede below (II), Laomedon and Capys with Priam, Hecuba, Tithonus, Hesione and Anchises below (III), to Hector, Paris, Cassandra, Aeneas and their kin (IV), the row of Agamemnon, and Astyanax (V). Aeacus, Peleus and Thetis lead to Achilles, and his son Neoptolemus marries Hermione, so Troy meets the house of Atreus through Helen, Cassandra and Hermione. The children of Heracles sit on Generation V: Hyllus and Macaria (by Deianira), the sons of Megara (a group: three in Apollodorus and Euripides, eight in Pindar), Telephus, Tlepolemus, and Iolaus, son of Iphicles; Iole and Omphale join Heracles’ row. Hyllus, Iolaus and Alcmene take their revenge on Eurystheus, and Achilles wounds and heals Telephus. Athens runs the earth-born Erichthonius (child of Hephaestus and Gaia, raised by Athena), with his son Pandion below him, and Europa (I); Erechtheus, Procne, Philomela and Tereus, the later Cecrops and Pandion, Minos and Pasiphae (II); Aegeus, Aethra, Itys, Androgeus, Ariadne, Phaedra, the Minotaur, Antiope and Pirithous, and the bandits of the Isthmus road (a group), with Theseus on a line below (III); Hippolytus and Demophon (IV). Theseus links to the Argonauts through Medea, Aegeus’ wife, and to Atreus through Helen, whom he carried off. The Odyssey runs Sisyphus (son of Aeolus), Autolycus (son of Hermes), Arcesius and Nausithous (II); Laertes and Anticleia, Icarius, Alcinous and Arete, and Telamon, son of Aeacus, with Hesione (III); Odysseus, Penelope, Nausicaa, Palamedes, Ajax, Polyphemus (son of Poseidon) and the companions of Odysseus (a group) (IV); Telemachus, Telegonus (his son by Circe) and the suitors of Penelope (a group) (V). Calypso, daughter of Atlas, sits on a line below him. The Sirens (a group, daughters of a Muse) and Scylla (daughter of Phorcys, or of Typhon and Echidna; a nymph Circe turned into a monster in Ovid) sit with the younger gods. Sisyphus is drawn as a second father of Odysseus, as Euripides, Sophocles and Hyginus tell it. A group’s card heads its member list with `count` and `members_label` (“A hundred and eight suitors”; “sisters” by default). The Seven against Thebes adds Calydon, Oeneus and Althaea (III) with Meleager, Deianira’s brother, and Atalanta (IV); Adrastus, Amphiaraus and Eriphyle, Tiresias and Haemon, son of Creon (V); the Seven with Polynices on Generation VI (Tydeus, Capaneus, Parthenopaeus) with Argia, Deipyle and Evadne; and a new row, Generation VII, for the Epigoni: Thersander, Diomedes (who wounds Aphrodite and Ares at Troy) and Alcmaeon. Bellerophon runs from Sisyphus through Glaucus of Corinth (on a line below him) and Iobates of Lycia (II), with Pegasus on a line below Medusa; Bellerophon, Philonoe, Proetus, Stheneboea and the Chimera (III); Hippolochus and Laodamia (IV); Glaucus of Lycia and Sarpedon, son of Zeus (V), who fight for Troy. Patroclus, Achilles’ friend, joins the row of Hector and kills Sarpedon. The house of Minos adds Asterion, Europa’s husband (I); Rhadamanthys, who marries Alcmene, Daedalus and Cocalus (II), with Catreus and Deucalion on a line below Minos; Glaucus and the seer Polyidus, Icarus and Perdix, Talos, and Catreus’ children Clymene and Althaemenes, with Nauplius (III); Idomeneus (IV). Aerope is Catreus’ daughter, so Crete reaches the house of Atreus; Clymene and Nauplius are the parents of Palamedes. Io and the Danaids runs from the river Inachus, son of Ocean (Children of the Titans), down a chain of lines inside Generation I: Io and Argus; Epaphus; Libya; Belus and Agenor (sons of Poseidon); Danaus, Aegyptus, Cepheus, Phineus, with Cadmus and Europa, Agenor’s children; the Danaids and the sons of Aegyptus (two groups of fifty), Hypermnestra, Lynceus and Amymone; Abas; Acrisius; Danaë. Amymone is the mother of Nauplius, Cepheus and Cassiopeia the parents of Andromeda; Proetus, Acrisius’ twin, stays on the row of Bellerophon. Generation I is tall in the whole tree because of this chain. The monsters adds Tartarus (first gods) and Pontus (Titans); Nereus, Phorcys, Ceto and Typhon (Children of the Titans; Typhon drops a line below Hera, who bears him alone in the Homeric Hymn to Apollo); Echidna, the Graeae, the Gorgons, the Nereids (groups) and Ladon (younger gods); Chrysaor below Medusa (II); Orthus and Geryon beside the Chimera (III); the Nemean Lion, the Hydra and Cerberus on the row of Heracles (IV); the Sphinx on the row of Oedipus (V). Parentage of the monsters is left as the sources give it, with Hesiod’s unclear ‘she’ noted on the link. Orpheus and the underworld adds Erebus, Night’s husband (first gods), her sons Hypnos and Thanatos (Titans), Tityos, son of Gaia (younger gods); Ixion, father of Pirithous (II); Oeagrus, Orpheus’ father, Pheres, son of Cretheus, and the Maenads (a group: Agave, Ino, Autonoe) (III); Eurydice, Admetus and Alcestis, daughter of Pelias (IV). The punishments in Hades (Tantalus, Sisyphus, Ixion, Tityos) are feuds with the god who imposed them. The Trojan War adds Eris, daughter of Night (Titans row), whose apple brings Hera and Athena’s hatred of Paris; Neleus, Pelias’ twin, Poeas, Oileus, Chryses and Eetion (III); Nestor with Antilochus below him, Memnon, son of Eos and Tithonus, Penthesilea, daughter of Ares, Philoctetes, Ajax the Lesser, Chryseis, Briseis, Helenus and Laocoon (IV), with the killings of Memnon, Penthesilea, Paris, Astyanax and the two Ajaxes’ fates. Prometheus and the fire adds Pandora, Epimetheus’ wife (Children of the Titans), and Deucalion of the flood (not the Cretan Deucalion), his wife Pyrrha and their son Hellen, father of Aeolus (with the younger gods): so the Titans reach the house of the Argonauts. Rows show a figure’s place in the story rather than a strict generation (Aphrodite, born of Uranus in Hesiod, sits with the younger gods). The Olympians are not a row: they span two, and are marked instead by a small peak beside the medallion (`data/olympians.json`). No poet in our source groups lists all twelve, so the list follows the Parthenon east frieze (Dionysus) and Ennius (Vesta = Hestia), with Plato’s Phaedrus for Hestia; the twelfth seat is hollow. The idea that Hestia gave her seat to Dionysus is modern; don’t add it. The page heading for the whole tree is "Gods and heroes" (it was "From Gaia to Orestes", which no longer fit once the tree reached Troy, the Argonauts and the Odyssey; "The gods and two cursed houses" sounded wrong before that).

## Current state

The site is live at https://ichor-orpin.vercel.app (Vite + D3, data in `/data`, deployed from `main` by Vercel). The original prototype, `prototype/ichor-atreidae.html`, stays as the reference for behaviour and tone.

- 342 figures, 803 relations, from Gaia and Uranus to Orestes and Antigone, with the house of Perseus down to Heracles, the house of Aeolus down to Jason and Medea, the house of Dardanus down to the fall of Troy, the children of Heracles, Theseus and Athens with the family of Minos, the Odyssey, the Seven against Thebes, Bellerophon, the house of Minos, Io and the Danaids, the monsters, Orpheus and the underworld, and the Trojan War, in rows computed from `generation`; a row grows a line downwards for a child whose parent shares the row (with its spouse), so lower always means descended; no staggering, which made height ambiguous; pan and pinch-zoom.
- **Source switcher**: All versions / Homer & epic / Hesiod / Lyric & tragedy / Apollodorus / Later writers. Links not attested in the chosen source fade out; a one-line note says what changes.
- **Layer toggles**: Bloodlines, Marriages, Affairs, Feuds & killings, Transformations, Curses.
- **Figure card**: Greek name, name, tagline, role, story, The Twelve (for Olympians), Born to / Married / Affairs / Feuds & killings / Transformations / Curses / Children (each with its sources), Where the sources disagree, In art.
- **Stories**: the two curses, how Zeus became king, and the myths of Cadmus, Hippodamia, Leda, Iphigenia, Perseus, the labours of Heracles, Theseus and the Minotaur, Jason and the Golden Fleece, the Seven against Thebes, the Trojan War, the wanderings of Odysseus, Prometheus and the fire, the house of Dardanus, Bellerophon and Pegasus, Minos and Daedalus, Io and the Danaids, Down to Hades, the children of Heracles, the loves of Zeus and the brood of Echidna (every family has at least one), told step by step, by all sources or as one source tells it, with “Versions differ” and “Read as” inline.
- **Ariadne’s thread**: the shortest path between two figures, respecting the chosen source.
- **Close family**: each card draws a small map of the figure’s nearest links (parents above, children below, husbands and wives to the left, lovers to the right), with feuds, curses and transformations on a ring around the figure below it, as the chosen source tells it, so versions show as extra parents; tapping a name moves to them. A prototype, chosen over a mind-map layout of the whole tree, which would lose fixed positions when the source changes.
- Light / dark theme switch; search with live filtering.

## Decisions made so far (can be revisited)

**Visual direction**
- Fonts: GFS Didot (display, names, stories, Greek) + Manrope (interface).
- Palette “pale clay and gold ichor” (chosen in task 4, replaces the first black-figure/red-figure palette). Light theme = black ink on pale, unfired clay. Dark theme = clay lines on black glaze. Follows the system setting until the reader picks a theme with the sun/moon button in the top right corner of the header; the pick is saved in localStorage (`ichor-theme`), and a pick that matches the system is not saved, so the page follows the system again. Tokens live in `src/styles/tokens.css`.
  - Gold is ichor, the blood of the gods: transformations, the selected figure, Ariadne’s thread, focus (`--accent`). Red is for curses only.
  - Light: bg `#E8CFB2`, panel `#F3E3D0`, rule `#C9A07D`, text `#1E140D`, lines `#2A1D14`, muted `#5A3F2C`, curse `#A82A17`, gold `#8A5E00`.
  - Dark: bg `#14100C`, panel `#1F1812`, rule `#3D3024`, text `#EAD3B8`, lines `#B98C66`, muted `#A68E76`, curse `#E0533C`, gold `#E8B54A`.
  - All text meets WCAG AA contrast on both bg and panel.
- Small medallion nodes: gods have a double ring, figures from other cycles a dashed ring, a diamond badge marks a transformation, a small gold star marks a mortal made immortal (Semele, Cadmus, Helen, Menelaus, the Dioscuri, Iphigenia, Heracles, Ino, Ganymede, Tithonus, Ariadne, Amphiaraus, Pegasus, Io, Memnon; it hides when the chosen source doesn’t tell it), a small peak marks one of the Twelve Olympians (hollow for Hestia and Dionysus); a group (the Muses, the Fates…) is a ringed medallion holding three small dots.
- Row names sit in a column pinned to the left edge of the screen (small caps, interface font, fixed size at any zoom); the tree slides beneath it and the names follow their rows up and down. The “Heroes and mortals” divider is named there too.
- Edge styles: descent = thin solid; marriage = double line; affair = dashed; feud = dash-dot; transformation = dotted gold with arrow; curse = red with arrow.
- Header (reworked in task 4; the meander band was dropped): a small caps kicker "Ichor · The family tree of Greek myth", then the family as the page title ("Gods and heroes" for the whole tree). The title is only a title: the family switcher is a pill on its own line, “Family [Whole tree ▾]”, just above the Source pills and labelled the same way, so both choices read as settings (chosen over a “Change family” button beside the title and over a framed title-button; the old “Now exploring” line and the chevron on the title were not obvious). On phones the Family and Source labels stay visible and the list opens full width. The families: Whole tree, The gods, The Titans, Zeus and his loves, Thebes, The House of Atreus, Perseus and Heracles, The Argonauts, Troy, The children of Heracles, Theseus and Athens, The Odyssey, The Seven against Thebes, Bellerophon and Corinth, The house of Minos, Io and the Danaids, Monsters and their killers, Orpheus and the underworld, The Trojan War (`families` in `data/branches.json`; the choice lives in the URL, `?family=thebes`). Everyone goes into the one pool of data; families keep the tree readable as it grows. Collectives are one medallion each (`kind: group`): the Muses, Moirai, Horae, Charites, Oceanids, Pleiades, Erinyes, the sons of Megara, the suitors of Penelope, the companions of Odysseus, the Danaids and the sons of Aegyptus, the Graeae, the Gorgons, the Nereids, the Maenads, the bandits of the Isthmus road, the Harpies, the Sirens, the Cyclopes (Hesiod’s three smiths, not the Cyclopes of the Odyssey) and the Hundred-Handers; the card lists the sisters. Sources are pills, the chosen one filled; on desktop their note sits on the same line.
- Tools: on desktop the layer toggles, Trace a curse, Ariadne’s thread and the search box float in a dock at the bottom of the tree; on phones four buttons (Layers, Curses, Thread, Find) float at the bottom and open panels in the card sheet. "Whole tree" sits in the top corner of the tree.

**Tone**
- Taglines read like a family drama ("Served his brother a dinner of his own children."). Stories: short sentences, past tense.
- All user-facing text in English, with typographic quotes and apostrophes.

**Sources**
- Groups: E = Homer, Homeric Hymns, Cypria; H = Hesiod (Theogony, Catalogue of Women); T = Pindar, Stesichorus, Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides; A = Apollodorus, Library; L = Pausanias, Hyginus, Ovid.
- Every relation must list its sources. Never add a link or a version without a source.

## Target structure

Keep it simple: a static site, no backend.

- Vite + vanilla TypeScript (or plain JS) + D3.
- Data out of the code, in `/data`:
  - `people.json` — id, name, greek, gender, generation, kind (god / nymph / mortal / other-cycle / group), branch, role, tagline, story, versions, transformation, immortal (text and sources), art; a group adds count, members and members_note.
  - `relations.json` — type, from, to, sources, note.
  - `stories.json` — stories told step by step: the two curses and further myths (`kind`: curse or story). A story marked `draft` still needs checking against the sources; how Zeus became king and the myths of Hippodamia, Leda, Iphigenia, Perseus, Heracles, Theseus, Jason, the Seven against Thebes, the Trojan War, the Odyssey, Prometheus, Dardanus, Bellerophon, Minos, Io, Hades, the children of Heracles, the loves of Zeus and the brood of Echidna have been checked. A step is told by a source when the links it reaches first (from its figures to people already met) are attested there; a step can list its own `pairs` when its figures reach further than it tells (Medea meets Jason in Colchis, not yet Pelias), and those links wait for their step on the tree. The camera frames the step’s figures and the other ends of the links that tell it.
  - `sources.json` — source groups and their labels.
- Styles as design tokens (CSS variables) in one file; components separate (tree, card, controls, curse panel, thread panel).
- Layout computed from data (generation tiers), so new branches can be added by editing JSON only.
- Deploy to Vercel.
